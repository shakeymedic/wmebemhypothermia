Assessment & Swiss Staging System

Accidental hypothermia is staged based on clinical findings and core temperature. Core temperature should be measured with an oesophageal or bladder probe. **Handle all patients gently** to avoid precipitating arrhythmias.

Stage I: Mild

35-32°C

Conscious & Shivering

  • Tachycardia, Tachypnoea
  • Increased metabolic rate
  • Amnesia, dysarthria
  • "Metabolic optimisation"

Stage II: Moderate

32-28°C

Impaired Consciousness, Shivering Ceases

  • Bradycardia, Hypotension
  • Reduced metabolic rate
  • Pupil dilation
  • Risk of arrhythmia (AF common)

Stage III: Severe

<28°C

Unconscious

  • Apnoea, asystole/VF possible
  • Areflexia
  • Loss of autoregulation
  • Significant risk of cardiac arrest

Stage IV: Arrest

<24°C (typically)

No Signs of Life

  • Clinically deceased
  • May be resuscitatable
  • See cardiac arrest management
Hypothermic Cardiac Arrest Management

"You are not dead until you are warm and dead."

Resuscitation Protocol

  • CPR: Continue high-quality chest compressions. Mechanical CPR is preferred.
  • Airway: Secure airway with ETT. Provide warm, humidified oxygen if possible.
  • Defibrillation: If in VF/VT, deliver up to 3 shocks. If unsuccessful, withhold further shocks until T > 30°C.
  • Drugs: Withhold all IV drugs until T > 30°C. Once >30°C, double the time interval between doses. Normal intervals when T > 35°C.
  • Rewarming: The priority is aggressive active core rewarming. This is the definitive treatment. ECLS (ECMO/CPB) is the method of choice.

Termination of Resuscitation

Consider stopping resuscitation if:

  • Obvious Lethal Injuries: Incompatible with life.
  • Asphyxiation: Known submersion >60 mins or avalanche victim with packed snow in airway.
  • Potassium > 12 mmol/L: Indicates cell lysis and non-viability. A sample must be taken *before* starting CPR if possible.
  • Failure to Rewarm: Despite adequate measures and ECLS.
  • Persistent Asystole: Once core temperature is >32°C.
HOPE Score: Predicting Survival

The Hypothermia Outcome Prediction after ECLS (HOPE) score is a validated tool to help predict survival in patients with hypothermic cardiac arrest. It can assist in the decision of whether to initiate or continue with ECLS.

Score Components

  • Sex: Male vs Female
  • Age: Per year
  • Asphyxia: Submersion, avalanche, or other causes
  • Serum Potassium: Measured in mmol/L
  • Core Temperature: At presentation
  • CPR Duration: Time from arrest to ECLS

Rewarming Methods

The choice of rewarming method depends on the patient's stability and degree of hypothermia. Rewarming can cause a paradoxical drop in core temperature ("afterdrop") and hypotension due to vasodilation.

Passive External Rewarming

For HT Stage I. Relies on patient's own heat generation.

Rate: ~0.5-1°C/hour

  • Remove all wet clothing.
  • Move to a warm, dry environment.
  • Cover with warm, dry blankets/layers.
  • Provide warm, sweet drinks if fully conscious.

Active External Rewarming

For HT Stage II. Can be used in Stage III if core rewarming is unavailable.

Rate: ~1-2.5°C/hour

  • Forced-air warming (e.g., Bair Hugger). Apply to torso to minimise afterdrop.
  • Warm water immersion (risk of profound hypotension).
  • Heated blankets/pads.

Active Core Rewarming

For HT Stage II-IV. Required for haemodynamically unstable patients.

Rate: Varies significantly

  • Warm IV fluids (40-42°C). Minimally effective for rewarming but prevents further cooling.
  • Warm, humidified inspired air (40-45°C).
  • Body cavity lavage (gastric, bladder, pleural, peritoneal). Moderately effective.
  • **Extracorporeal Life Support (ECLS):** Veno-arterial ECMO or Cardiopulmonary Bypass. The most effective method (~10°C/hour), essential in cardiac arrest.
